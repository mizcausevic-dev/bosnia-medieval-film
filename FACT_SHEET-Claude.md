# Fact sheet: "Manuscript into place" (Charter of Ban Kulin, 1189)

Status: APPROVED by Miz 2026-10-19 (typographic, no facsimile). Rev 2 after independent review: rows 11 and 12 added, wording rules updated. Nothing in the film may go beyond this sheet.
Prepared 2026-10-19. Evidence grades: **Verified** (two or more independent sources, or the document's own colophon as rendered by a scholar), **Attributed** (one source, stated as that source's claim), **Contested**, **Unverified** (do not show).

## The one idea
On 29 August 1189, Ban Kulin of Bosnia put a promise to the people of Dubrovnik in writing: safe passage and trade in his lands, and his help while they were there.

## Source quality (read this first)
- **Best source:** Zenaida Karavdić, "O Povelji Kulina bana: Bez' v'sega zloga primysla", Sarajevo, c. 2009 (the text says "exactly 820 years" since 1189). PDF hosted at godisnjak.anubih.ba. A linguistic study with a full modern translation, the copy readings, and a bibliography. I extracted and read the text. The journal title was not visible in the extraction.
- **Secondary, checked:** Wikipedia (Charter of Ban Kulin, Ban Kulin, Banate of Bosnia, Stećak); Zemaljski muzej BiH page; Ramić 2019 abstract (journal *Uprava*, vol. 10 no. 1); RFE/RL article by Marija Arnautović (28 Aug 2009); N1 (29 Aug 2024).
- **Not obtained:** a critical edition or facsimile. The edition to consult is *Osamsto godina Povelje bosanskog bana Kulina 1189–1989*, ANUBiH Posebna izdanja knj. 23, Sarajevo 1989, plus J. Vrana, "Da li je sačuvan original isprave Kulina bana?", *Radovi Staroslavenskog instituta* 2 (1955), 5–57. Both are cited in Karavdić. I have not read either.
- **Blocked:** the UNESCO page returned HTTP 403, so UNESCO stećci figures are not used in this film.

## Contested framing, handled explicitly
- **Superlatives:** the sources call it the "oldest preserved written monument in Old Bosnian" (Karavdić), "Bosnia's first international treaty" (Ramić), or a "birth certificate" (press). These are interpretations. **Film rule: no "oldest", "first" or "birth certificate".** Use only "from 1189".
- **Ownership:** Karavdić says that since the 1990s the charter has been used for political ends, producing interpretations that are not scholarly. A search result headline showed a competing national claim to the language (I did not read that article). **Film rule: the film names no nation or language as owner. It says "a Slavic vernacular text in Cyrillic" and "Bosnian Cyrillic" (the script's scholarly name).** A one-line credit card states that interpretations of the charter's place in national histories are contested.
- **Statehood framing:** the headline "evidence of centuries-old Bosnian statehood" is N1's interpretation. The film shows an act by a ruler and does not argue a thesis.
- **Not used (kept out on purpose):** the Bilino Polje abjuration (1203), Kulin's "golden age" folk memory, the Vlach and Bogomil debates about stećci, the 1817 and 1832 provenance story, the UNESCO figures.
